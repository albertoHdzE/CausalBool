# Bitacora 04 — Cellular Automaton to Network by Deconvolution

Date: 2026-07-09
Status: complete and verified

## Aim

Apply the invented index-set deconvolution to the setting of Zenil and
colleagues: given the space-time evolution of a cellular automaton over a period
of time, recover a Boolean network that reproduces that behaviour. The rules used
are those of the imp-causal-paper reproduction (254, 90, 30, 110, 232, 204, 250,
150, 170, 57, 45, 73).

## The equivalence: a cellular automaton is a Boolean network

An elementary cellular automaton is already a synchronous Boolean network. Each
cell is a node; node i is connected to its neighbourhood {i-1, i, i+1} (periodic
boundary); and every node carries the same local gate, namely the rule's
three-input Boolean function. Wolfram rule numbering maps directly to a truth
table: the output for neighbourhood (l, c, r) is bit 4l+2c+r of the rule number.
So the CausalBool formalism already contains the elementary cellular automata,
with a circulant connectivity matrix and a homogeneous gate.

Some rules coincide with named gates: rule 254 is OR over the three cells, rule
232 is MAJORITY, rule 150 is three-input XOR, rule 90 is XOR of the two outer
cells with the centre irrelevant, rule 204 is the identity on the centre, rule
170 is a shift. Rules such as 30 and 110 have no canonical name and are carried
as explicit look-up tables. All of these are recovered correctly (below).

## The challenge and its resolution

A space-time diagram is not the exhaustive input repertoire. It is a trajectory:
each row is the input to the next, so we observe the local rule only on the
neighbourhoods that happen to occur. Two consequences follow.

1. Deconvolution here is the per-node method of Bitacora 01 applied to
   trajectory samples rather than to the full repertoire. For each cell we pool
   the samples (state -> next value), find the smallest neighbourhood radius that
   explains them deterministically (the paper's "smallest rule that causally
   disconnects regions"), drop any neighbour proven irrelevant, build the local
   truth table, and name the gate against the canonical family with a look-up
   table fall-back.

2. A single trajectory may not exercise every neighbourhood. A saturating rule
   such as 254 fills the lattice with ones after two steps, so a single diagram
   only shows the all-ones neighbourhood and the recovered model, while
   reproducing that trajectory, is not the true rule. The resolution is to pool
   an ensemble of observations from several random initial conditions until every
   local neighbourhood has been seen. This is the constructive counterpart of the
   user's second route: instead of one orbit we gather enough of the automaton's
   behaviour to determine the local rule completely. Coverage is reported per
   cell.

The decisive test is therefore not trajectory reproduction (which is easy and
always achievable) but exact recovery of the global map: the reconstructed
network's exhaustive repertoire must equal the automaton's next-state map over
all 2^w states.

## Results

Experiment `experiments/exp03_ca_to_network.py`, width 12, 10 steps, 80 initial
conditions per rule.

- Trajectory exact: 12 / 12 rules.
- Global map exact: 12 / 12 rules.

Recovered local structure for the interior cell:

| Rule | Support size | Gate recovered | Identity |
|---|---|---|---|
| 254 | 3 | OR | OR of the three cells |
| 90 | 2 | XOR | XOR of the outer cells, centre dropped |
| 232 | 3 | MAJORITY | majority of the three |
| 150 | 3 | XOR | XOR of the three cells |
| 204 | 1 | buffer | identity on the centre |
| 170 | 1 | buffer | shift (one outer cell) |
| 250 | 2 | OR | OR of the outer cells, centre dropped |
| 30, 110, 45, 57, 73 | 3 | LUT | no canonical name |

The single-input buffers (rules 204, 170) are named AND by the canonical
priority, which is the arity-one identity; this is the equivalence-class naming
already documented in Bitacora 02.

## Verification and the Wolfram notebook

The method is implemented in Python (`src/ca_deconvolution.py`) and ported to
Wolfram Language (`src/CADeconvolution.wl`). The two agree: the Wolfram port
recovers the exact global map on 254, 90, 232, 150, 30, 110, with the same gate
identities and supports.

The notebook `experiments/ca_to_network_demo.nb` runs a rule-90 walkthrough
(showing the centre dropped and the gate named XOR over a support of two cells)
and then recovers every example rule, confirming exact global-map recovery. It
is generated by `experiments/build_ca_notebook.wl` and verified by
`crosscheck/verify_ca_notebook.wl`, which extracts and evaluates its input cells
(emulating Evaluate Notebook) and reports 0 messages with both stages exact.

Tests: `tests/test_deconvolution.py` includes CA cases; the full suite is 11 / 11.

## Significance

The deconvolution invented for the index-set method transfers cleanly to the
cellular-automaton setting of the algorithmic-information literature, but with a
stronger guarantee: where that literature recovers a generating rule
approximately (through an approximate complexity), here the recovery is exact and
is certified by exhaustive global-map equivalence. The CA-to-network direction
also demonstrates the equivalence of Wolfram rules and Boolean gates concretely
(OR, XOR, MAJORITY, identity, shift, and look-up tables for the rest) and the
functional-versus-structural distinction in a new guise: rule 90 has a
structural neighbourhood of three cells but a functional support of two.

## Open questions

1. Recover the local rule from a single trajectory by actively constructing the
   missing neighbourhood inputs (the user's second route), rather than pooling
   random initial conditions, and quantify the minimal observation needed.
2. Extend to larger neighbourhood radii and to second-order (two-row) rules,
   where a single-row support is insufficient.
3. Relate the minimal-support inference to the pivots/sumandos description length
   and to a formal identifiability theorem for homogeneous local networks.
