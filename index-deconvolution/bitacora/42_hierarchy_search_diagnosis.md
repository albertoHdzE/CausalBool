# Where HID-search-v2 loses: search reach versus representation cost

Date: 2026-10-03. Executor: Claude Code. Supervisor: Codex. Status: **ready for Codex
review; not yet accepted.** Evidence role: post-hoc diagnostic on already inspected data.

The accepted HID-search-v2 study (bitacora 41) was inconclusive against the nine-codec
portfolio. This phase asked which concrete limitation deserves the next algorithm
proposal, through four fixed diagnostics on the saved run, without new data.

The saved-result map (D1) re-read all 1,792 cases and reproduced the accepted primary
estimate exactly. Giving the boundary stage eight times its caps (D2) changed its output
on one of 176 target strings and none of 32 controls; the frozen stage itself replayed
bit for bit. Pricing every subset of the generator's supplied cuts (D3, truth-assisted)
showed that, in all 72 boundary-family strings where the full method loses, the unchanged
leaf language holds a partition shorter than both the full archive and the portfolio;
mostly the obstacle is which cuts B proposes, with 22 strings showing a positive-cost
barrier on the strict path. Rewriting the saved period and pair-grammar archives in HID
(D4) found no translation shorter than the full archive, and every translation longer
than its baseline; in 282 of the 367 losses to those codecs the full archive already has
exactly the translated length, so the loss is the HID cost of the same proposal.

Recommendation: `BOTH_SEPARATELY` — a boundary-proposal change for F12/S02 and a
representation change for the period-type families, as separate studies. F06–F11, where
the largest per-bit losses sit, were not examined.
