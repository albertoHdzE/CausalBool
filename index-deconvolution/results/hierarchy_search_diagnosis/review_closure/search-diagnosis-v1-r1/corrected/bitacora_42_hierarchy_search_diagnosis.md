The accepted HID-search-v2 study (bitacora 41) was inconclusive against the nine-codec
portfolio. This phase asked which concrete limitation deserves the next algorithm
proposal, through four fixed diagnostics on the saved run, without new data.

The saved-result map (D1) re-read all 1,792 cases and reproduced the accepted primary
estimate exactly. Giving the boundary stage eight times its caps (D2) changed its output
on one of 176 target strings and none of 32 controls — a result about that one cap
increase; the frozen stage itself replayed bit for bit. Pricing every subset of the
generator's supplied cuts (D3, truth-assisted) showed that, in all 72 boundary-family
strings where the full method loses, the unchanged leaf language holds a partition
shorter than both the full archive and the portfolio, with 22 strings showing a
positive-cost barrier on the strict path. Which mechanism stops B is not identified:
proximity of supplied cuts to the cuts of B's returned archive says nothing about the
cuts it proposed. Rewriting the saved period and pair-grammar archives in HID (D4) found
no translation shorter than the full archive, and every translation longer than its
baseline; in 282 of the 367 losses to those codecs the full archive has exactly the
translated length (261 with identical bytes, 21 without), so there the loss equals that
translation's measured penalty — not a proof that no better HID description exists.

Recommendation, exploratory: `BOTH_SEPARATELY` — a boundary-search change for F12/S02 and
a representation change for the period-type families, as separate studies. The losses of
F06–F11, the largest per bit, were not targeted: F06, F07 and F11 appear only as D2
controls, F08–F10 only in D1.
